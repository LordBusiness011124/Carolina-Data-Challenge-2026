Sources & calculation methods

Each lookup uses coordinates and geographic identifiers. Provider responses are cached for up to 24 hours; climate data and the global archive for seven days.

What these numbers mean

Observed annual occurrence is the fraction of complete calendar years with at least one hurricane-classified track position within 50 miles, using NOAA's USA wind and category fields. It is a historical sample frequency, not a forecast or an insured-loss probability. Track positions are discrete, so a storm passing between observations can be missed.

FEMA exposure is the county-wide building value exposed to hurricanes in an archived risk model, not a prediction of damage. The risk rating is FEMA's relative county ranking. ACS enrollment counts resident K–12 students, including private-school students; it is not enrollment at the NCES public schools shown on the map. ACS values are survey estimates.

Climate changes compare model means for 2025–2034 and 2040–2049 against 1995–2014. One model (MRI-AGCM3-2-S) is shown under HighResMIP forcing close to RCP8.5; it is not an ensemble or a hurricane-probability projection. Closures, unemployment spikes, and recovery times require event-specific evidence and are not inferred from these sources.

For locations outside U.S. county coverage, the assessment shows global storm history and climate context. The U.S. community datasets are not extrapolated to other countries.
